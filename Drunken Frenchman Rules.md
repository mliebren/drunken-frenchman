Materials:

54 card standard card deck with 2 jokers included.



Game Start:

&#x20;1) Shuffle the cards.

&#x20;2) Each player is dealt 13 cards.

&#x20;3) Each player looks at and sorts their hand. If one or both players do not have at least one special card, the hand is considered a "Hand of Crap", and the cards are re-shuffled and re-dealt.



Betting for the first turn:

&#x20;1) The rest of the cards are placed in the draw pile face down, which lies in between the two players.

&#x20;2) The top card is revealed.

&#x20;3) Each player "bets" on who will go first once the game starts.

&#x09; a. Each player chooses one card, typically the highest value non-special card they possess which does not fit into a set.

&#x09; b. When both players are ready, their "bet" cards are revealed.

&#x09; c. The player with the highest value card wins.

&#x09; 	i. Aces are high, suits are determined in reverse alphabetical order - 1) Spades 2) Hearts 3) Diamonds 4) Clubs

&#x09; d. The winning player may choose one of the three cards which are face up (including the top card of the draw pile, and the two cards which were bet) OR they may draw from the draw pile. 

&#x09; e. All other cards which were not selected are moved to the BOTTOM of the draw pile.

&#x20;	 This counts as the first draw of the game.



Turns:

&#x20;Each turn follows three key steps:

&#x20;1) Draw one card from draw pile OR one or more cards from the discard pile\* to start the turn (excluding the very first turn, which is determined by the winner of the bet).

&#x20;2) Play cards. If you have a set of cards which can be played, you MUST play them. This excludes plays which involve special cards.

&#x09; - During the play phase, a player must play all cards from their hand that can legally be played without using a special-card ability. Mandatory plays are resolved one at a time, with the player choosing among available legal plays. After each play, the game reevaluates the player's hand and the table for newly available mandatory plays. This continues until no mandatory plays remain. A player may not voluntarily stop while a mandatory play is available.

&#x20;3) Discard into the discard pile. Discarding is mandatory and must be done to end the player's turn.

&#x09; - At the end of each turn, a player must discard exactly one card from their hand face-up into the discard pile, unless a game-ending condition or special-card effect alters the turn. A player may not discard a card that is eligible for a mandatory play. A player may not discard their final card to go out; going out requires playing their final card onto a legal group.

&#x20;4) The game ends when a player discards their last card. If you play all of your cards and are unable to discard, you must undo your last play and discard one of the cards played.

&#x09; - A player goes out when their hand reaches zero cards after resolving all mandatory plays and the applicable special-card effects for that play. Going out initiates an end-of-game resolution state, not an immediate game end. During this state, the opponent may use eligible black 2 abilities under the designated end-of-game response rules. If the game is successfully continued, the player who went out returns to the active game state.



The game ends when one player "goes out" - this means they played all of their cards. The last card played is discarded face down. If the player who still has cards plays a black two to make the player draw 7 cards, this card is picked up along with 6 more cards from the discard and then draw pile, and the game continues. If the second player who did not go our does not have a black two to play, the game truly ends, and scoring begins.



Discard pile:

&#x20;- The discard pile will grow and shrink as the game progresses. 

&#x20;- Cards in the discard pile are flayed out face up to save space, but still revealing the number and suit of each card in the pile.

&#x20;- Players can draw from the discard pile during the opening of their turn in two ways:

&#x09;1) Draw the top card of the discard pile.

&#x09;	a) Example: Discard pile is, from bottom to top, AH, QD, JC, 4S, 9D. The player draws 9D. The player does not have to play this card during their turn.

&#x09;2) Draw from any point in the discard pile. The bottom card drawn MUST be played. All cards above it in the discard pile are moved to the player's hand as if they were drawn normally. These do not have to be played.

&#x09;	a) Example: Discard pile is, from bottom to top, AH, QD, JC, 4S, 9D. The player draws JC and above - this means they MUST play JC during their turn, and 4S and 9D are drawn into their hand.

&#x09;3) Rarely, the game will continue through when all of the draw pile is empty. The player drawing then must then draw one card from the opposing player's hand, blindly.



Playing cards:

Cards may be played, or placed face up on the table in groups, in one of the two following patterns:

1\) A "run" of 3 or more. A run is a sequential sequence of the same suit of card. Aces are high only, and thus you may NOT have a run which "loops" from King to Ace to 2. You may have a run of up to 13 cards.

2\) A "set" of 3 (or 4) is a collection of three and up to four of the same value (including face cards and Aces).

Where Drunken Frenchman differs from similar games (Gin Rummy, etc.) is that you CAN play your cards on your existing run or sets OR the opposing player's run or set. 

Examples:

&#x09;i. If player 1 has three 7's and player 1 later draws the fourth seven, they MUST play the fourth seven. If they play the fourth seven on their own set, it is added to the set. No more cards can be added to this set, since it is a complete set of four.

&#x09;ii. If player 1 has three 7's and player 2 later draws the fourth seven, they MUST play the fourth seven. This is added to player 2's side of the table and counts to player 2's points. It still correlates with the set, and no more cards can be played onto this set.

&#x09;iii. If player 1 has a run of 9H 10H JH and they have KH in their hand, then they later draw QH, they then MUST play QH AND KH (in the continued ascending order) onto the existing set.

&#x09;iv. If player 1 plays a run of 9H 10H JH on their turn, and player 2 has QH in their hand, player 2 MUST play the QH on their turn. This is added to player 2's side of the table and counts to player 2's points. It still correlates with the run, and more cards can be played on that run by either player.

&#x09;v. Assuming example iv. occurs, and player 1 has a KH in their hand, they then MUST play the KH on their turn. This is added to the run, and is played within the grouping of the run on the table. It still correlates with the run, and more cards can be played on that run by either player.

&#x09;vi. Assuming example iv and v both occur, and player 2 draws an AH, they then MUST play the AH on their turn. This is added to player 2's side of the table and counts to player 2's points, and is played within the grouping of the run on the table (in player 2's case, it will appear that they have played QH and AH in a run, but this is a continuation of player 1's run, and is therefore legal.



Special cards:

Drunken Frenchman also differs from similar games as it has three types of special cards. Special cards may be played as "themselves", but do not have to be, as outlined in Turns Rule 2. The three\* types of special cards are:



2's (both colors)

Once a 2 is played AS A SPECIAL CARD it is exhausted from the game. It is not added to the discard or draw piles and may not be played or picked up. 2s can be played at any time. It does not have to be played during your turn.

&#x09;Red (Hearts, Diamonds) 2's: 

&#x09;	 - May be used to "steal" one played group (set or run) from the other player's played table, as is their choice. This moves the stolen group to the player who played the red 2's side, and count towards their points. 

&#x09;	 - If the group being stolen is a subset of another group, i.e. a continuation of a run or a set, that subset is added to the larger group. That group is now all combined. 

&#x09;	 - In example iv. above, player two having already have QH played, they may choose to steal the 9H 10H JH run. This is then combined with the QH into a new group, 9H 10H JH QH, which all count toward player 2's score.

&#x09;	 - Red 2s CANNOT be played after the game ends. If player one goes out, and player 2 does not have a black two to continue the game, they may NOT play a red two to try to steal cards after the fact.
		 - If the group being stolen is a subset of another group, that subset is added to the larger group. That group is now all combined.

&#x09;	 - Two red twos can be played simultaneously. If this happens, only one of the red twos can be countered by a black two, and the other may not be countered.

&#x09;	 - Groups are defined by their current structural composition, not by their original play. A red 2 targets one currently recognized group. If that group is a valid subset or continuation of another group, the affected cards are merged into the resulting group according to the applicable run/set rules. Ownership of each card is transferred individually to the player using the red 2, unless a specific rule states otherwise.

&#x09;Black (Aces, Clubs) 2's: 

&#x09;	 - These can be used in one of two ways:

&#x09;	1) Counteract a red 2. If player 1 plays a red two, player 2 MAY immediately play a black 2 to counteract the red two. 

&#x09;		The card stolen from player 2 is returned to their side of the table PLUS they get to steal one group of cards from player 1. It is as if they have played 2 red twos, and the same grouping rules apply.

&#x09;		 - If player 1 then plays ANOTHER red two, the pattern continues. They get to take back both groups of cards, AND get to steal ANOTHER group of cards.

&#x09;		 - If player 2 then plays ANOTHER black two after the second red 2 by player 1, the pattern continues. They get all three groups of cards, plus a fourth group.

&#x09;	2) Make the other player draw 7 cards. 

&#x09;		This can be played at any time, INCLUDING after the opposing player has gone out. 

&#x09;		This continues the game as if the player who had gone out had simply discarded (after they draw the card they discarded to "go out" (face down)), plus six more.

&#x09;		 - Cards drawn after a black 2 are drawn in the following order 

&#x09;			1) The last discarded card (if the player receiving the punishment has gone out)

&#x09;			2) They draw cards from the discard pile until either the discard pile is empty or they reach seven cards drawn.

&#x09;			3) If the discard pile is empty, the player begins drawing from the draw pile until they reach seven cards.

&#x09;				a) Rarely, the game will continue through when all of the draw pile is empty. The player then must begin drawing cards from the other player's hand, blindly, until they have drawn seven cards total.

&#x09;				b) If less than seven cards are available across all sources, the game ends in a tie, and all 

&#x09;	When a player goes out, the opponent receives a defined response window to play a black 2. If no black 2 is played during that window, the game ends. If a black 2 is played, the player who went out must draw seven cards using the prescribed draw order, and the game resumes. The black 2 is exhausted upon activation. Any subsequent mandatory plays are resolved during the resumed turn according to the established turn rules.

8's of any color: 

&#x20;- Used to steal one card from either player's played cards. The 8 played specially is then substituted with that card and no longer counts for points. It does, however, still count towards the grouping it substituted within. The card substituted MUST be played immediately on the same turn in which it was substituted.

&#x20;- Example:  If player 1 has a run of 9H 10H JH, on player 2's turn, they MAY substitute one or more of the cards in the run to aid in creating their own group, a set. 

&#x09;	On player 2's turn, they have two Jacks, and need a third to create a set, so they steal the JH from player 1 using an 8 of any color, in this case 8C, and play JH JC JD.

&#x09;	- Player 1 now has a set which reads 9H 10H 8C. The 8 is simply a substitute, so QH may be played on this group for example, and it would still be tied to this group.



&#x20;- Example: If player 1 has a set of 9H 9D 9C, on player 2's turn, they MAY substitute one or more of the cards in the run to aid in creating their own group, a run. 

&#x09;	On player 2's turn, they have 10H and JH and need a third to create a set, so they steal the 9H from player 1 using an 8 of any color, in this case 8C, and play 9H 10H JH.

&#x09;	- Player 1 now has a set which reads 8C 9D 9C. The 8 is simply a substitute, so 9S may be played on this group for example, and it would still be tied to this group.



An 8 may be used to take any card at any index from the draw pile on a player's turn. 

The 8 is substituted for the card in the draw pile and the card taken then MUST be played immediately. The 8 can later be drawn as normal by either player and either played as an 8 itself or used as a special card.



Jokers: 

&#x09; - Jokers serve as wild cards and can be played as any other card. 

&#x09; - Jokers may not be played as another special card, so you can't play a joker as a red 2 and steal a group, for example.

&#x09; - Jokers may be substituted with an 8. This causes the player who substituted the card to choose a new wild assignment for the joker as they please.

&#x09;	 - For example, player 1 has 7H JOKER 9H played on the table. Player 2 has QD and KD in their hand. 

&#x09;		On player 2's turn, they may use an 8 to substitute with the Joker and play that same Joker as the AD or JD as they choose. 

&#x09;		Player 1's played group now shows 7H 8(any) 9H, and player 2's played group now reads QD KD JOKER.

&#x09;	 - This can then be taken again and played again by either player as a new (or the same) card.

&#x09;- Jokers can be taken from a set and played onto the same set as long as the total number of cards in that set stays under 5.

&#x09;	 - For example, if player 1 has AH AD JOKER, and player 2 wishes to take JOKER from the set, they MAY play the joker back on the set, as it represents not the same card, but a new Ace in the set.

&#x20;		 - Logically, the Joker in player 1's original hand represents EITHER AC or AS, and when it is stolen, that quantum behavior remains with the 8, so placing the new Joker is not playing AC or AS, it is playing simply the fourth Ace.

&#x09; - Cards may be played onto sets with quantum Jokers, and would simply turn the quantum Joker into the suit of the last card. 

&#x09;	 - In our example above, if player 1 has AH AD JOKER and either player wishes to play AS or AC or another Joker on this set, they may, because the count is not greater than four. 

&#x09;	 - The Joker in player 1's hand then no longer represents a third Ace, it represents the last Ace (if AC is played, it represents AS and vis versa).


Duplicate Cards:

&#x09; - Cards may be duplicated across a single set or group. This happens when an 8 is played and a card is substituted with the 8.

&#x09; - Sets may not have more than one of the same card, and may not have more than four cards total. If a card is taken from a set, it may not be played back on the same set, because it is already present in the set, just replaced by an 8.

&#x09;	 - For example, if player 1 has AH AD AC AS, and player 2 wishes to take AS from the set, they may not play it back onto the same set on their side, as there would be a duplication of AS.

&#x09;	 - This is true for player 1 as well, they may not play a joker on this set as it is complete nor take a card from this set and play it back on the set. 

&#x09; -Each played card belongs to exactly one group at a time. A card may not simultaneously be part of multiple sets or runs. Cards substituted by an 8 remain associated with the group in which the substitution occurred.



Scoring

&#x20;Each player at the end of the game is given a score based on the value of cards they have played minus the value of the cards in their hand. There is a +20 point bonus for going out in a way that ends the game (a black 2 is not played). Ownership is by card, whatever is on the table in front of that player, regardless of group, counts towards their score.

Cards played (on the table):

&#x09;2 - 9:       5pts

&#x09;10, J, Q, K: 10pts

&#x09;A:           15 pts

Cards not played (in a player's hand)

&#x09;2 - 9:       -5pts

&#x09;10, J, Q, K: -10pts

&#x09;A:           -15 pts



Jokers are worth the value of the card they represent. 

&#x09; - For example if player 1 has a set of 10H 10D JOKER, the group would be worth 30 points total. 

&#x09; - For example if player 1 has a set of 4D 5D JOKER, the group would be worth 15 points total.

&#x09; - Jokers which have not been played are worth -10 points

8s which are played as special cards do not count for any points.

&#x09;- For example if player 1 has a set of 10H 10D 8C, the group would be worth 20 points total.

&#x09;- For example if player 1 has a set of 4D 5D 8D, the group would be worth 10 points total.

&#x09;- 8s which have not been played are worth -5 points.

2s which have been played are exhausted and do not count for any points. 2s which have not been played count for -5 points.



There are two ways of scoring: cumulative and single-game. In single-game, the player with the most points at the end wins. In cumulative, score is kept track of and the first player to a decided-on number wins, OR games can continue forever, counting cumulative score as you go.
